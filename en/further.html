2Watch list and bridges

To continue in your own field (computing, chemistry, geosciences, health, telecoms). Links checked on 18 October 2026.

  • UNESCO — Free online quantum computing course, with mentorship for up to 50 African women PhD students in science (announced July 2025).
  • IBM Quantum Learning — Free quantum computing courses with Qiskit.
  • PennyLane — Quantum computing tutorials and courses, including chemistry and machine learning applications.
  • QWorld — Network of introductory quantum computing workshops.
  • Africa Quantum Consortium — African consortium: actors and publications, to be found on the site.
  • SA QuTI — South African quantum technology initiative (universities, training, news).
  • QuVis — Interactive simulations from the University of St Andrews, used with this course.

3Reading texts on quantum and spirituality with care

The notebook’s rule: quantum physics proves no spirituality, and no spirituality proves quantum physics. Each source below calls for a different level of caution.

SourceTypeSuggested use
Gade, C. B. N. (2012), “What is Ubuntu? Different Interpretations among South Africans of African Descent”, South African Journal of Philosophy 31(3), 484–503Peer-reviewed academic articleSurveys several readings of ubuntu (moral quality, philosophy, worldview, ethics, African humanism). Does not deal with physics: read it to avoid reducing ubuntu to a single meaning.
Ramose, M. B. (1999), African Philosophy through Ubuntu, Harare, Mond BooksPhilosophy bookA reference for the philosophical context of ubuntu. Not to be used as a physics source.

(2024), “What does modern quantum physics have in common with…” (full title to be checked), journal JISS 5(1), doi:10.10520/ejc-jiss_v5_n1_a3Journal articlePublished comparative analysis of African traditional spirituality and quantum physics. To be read as a starting point for debate, not as an authority: apply the notebook’s rule and the three questions. Only the abstract could be consulted when this page was written.
Mokgoro, I., “Quantum Ubuntu and the Future of African Migration”, Medium, 2025Popular essay, non-academicUses “entanglement” as a metaphor for migration and the tie between diaspora and community; cites no experiment. To be read as ethical reflection, never as a physics argument.
