Read the transcript

Send photons one at a time towards two slits. Each one lands at a single point on the screen, like a particle. Yet after a few thousand impacts, fringes appear, as they would for a wave.

To describe this, we do not add probabilities. We give each path an amplitude, which is a complex number. When the paths are indistinguishable, we add the amplitudes, then take the squared modulus. This is the Born rule. Two opposite amplitudes cancel out, and the fringe is dark.

As soon as a physical trace reveals which path was taken, the fringes fade. This is also what happens to Schrödinger's cat: its surroundings observe it all the time. We call this decoherence.

The qubit puts this idea to work for information. Its state is written alpha zero plus beta one, where the modulus of alpha squared plus the modulus of beta squared equals one. We represent it as a point on the Bloch sphere. Be careful, though. A measurement gives only one outcome, and a qubit never yields more than one bit of classical information.

1.1Single-quantum-object interference

Einstein“God does not play dice. Every particle must have a well-defined position and velocity, even if we do not know them.”

Bohr“Albert, stop telling God what to do. A quantum object is neither a wave nor a particle: it shows us one face or the other depending on the question we ask it.”

In 1801, Thomas Young sends light through two slits: bright and dark fringes appear, the signature of a wave. Let us repeat the experiment, sending the photons one at a time. Classical intuition predicts two spots, one behind each slit. That is not what we observe.

  • Each photon arrives at a precise point on the detector, like a particle.
  • The first impacts look scattered at random: we cannot predict where the next one will land.
  • After a few thousand impacts, the fringes reappear.
Principle 1.1 — Indistinguishable paths

Interference appears when several indistinguishable paths lead to the same detection. The more a physical trace makes the path knowable, the more the fringes fade; they vanish completely when the path can be determined perfectly.

The Mach-Zehnder interferometer

To control the paths precisely, we use two 50/50 beam splitters (BS), two mirrors (M) and a phase plate that adds a phase shift \(\varphi = 2\pi\,\Delta\ell/\lambda\) on one arm. The lecture notes describe four set-ups:

  • MZ1 and MZ2: the paths remain distinguishable. 50% / 50% (or 25% at each output), no interference.
  • MZ3 (balanced interferometer, \(\varphi = 0\)): every photon leaves through the same detector.
  • MZ4 (unbalanced interferometer): \(P(D_x) = \cos^2(\varphi/2)\) and \(P(D_y) = \sin^2(\varphi/2)\).

Interference of a single photon in a Mach-Zehnder was cleanly demonstrated by Grangier, Roger and Aspect in 1986, with a source that truly emits only one photon at a time. In 1999, Arndt and Zeilinger’s team in Vienna observed interference with fullerene molecules C60: 60 carbon atoms, about 1,080 nucleons and electrons.

Analogy to critique — Kirikou Odyssey

Imagine a video game inspired by Dogon cosmology, in which the hero exists both in the living state (he pushes rocks: the particle aspect) and in the spirit state (he passes through obstacles and occupies several places: the wave aspect). Some objects in the game force him to “choose” a single reality.

Where does the analogy break down?

In the game, the result of the “choice” is written in the code: it is determined. A quantum measurement generally gives a genuinely random outcome, of which only the probability is fixed (unless the state is already an eigenstate of the measured quantity). Another limit: the hero cannot interfere with himself. Yet it is the interference between the two “possibilities” that is the quantum signature.

1.2Probability amplitudes

A probability is a positive real number: two probabilities cannot cancel each other. To describe a dark fringe, we need an object that adds or subtracts, like a wave. So we associate a complex number with each path: the amplitude.

Definitions 1.2 — State vector and amplitude

The state of a system is described by a ket \(\ket{\psi}\). The transition from a state \(\ket{i}\) to a state \(\ket{f}\) is described (in the absence of any intermediate evolution) by the complex amplitude

$$\mathcal{A}(f \leftarrow i) = \braket{f|i}.$$

Born rule: \(P(f \leftarrow i) = |\braket{f|i}|^2\).

The rules of the game

Paths in parallel (indistinguishable): add the amplitudes. Steps in series: multiply the amplitudes. Probability: take the squared modulus of the total amplitude.

Back to the Mach-Zehnder

A beam splitter transmits with amplitude \(1/\sqrt2\) and reflects with amplitude \(i/\sqrt2\) (a phase shift of \(\pi/2\) on reflection). Following the four paths of MZ4, the lecture notes obtain

$$\mathcal A(x) = -e^{i\varphi/2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},\qquad \mathcal A(y) = -e^{i\varphi/2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2},\qquad P(x) + P(y) = 1.$$

Going further

When random disorder makes these cancellations systematic, the electron stays trapped: this is Anderson localisation (Nobel Prize 1977).

Analogy to critique — quantum Ndolé

In a well-made Ndolé, the bitterness of the leaves and the sweetness of the groundnuts offset or reinforce each other depending on the quantities and the moment they are added. Quantity ↔ modulus of the amplitude; moment ↔ phase.

Where does the analogy break down?

Two flavours never cancel each other completely: neither is worth “minus one”. Amplitudes, on the other hand, can be exactly opposite and give zero probability. And a recipe followed carefully always gives the same dish, whereas a carefully prepared quantum measurement gives random outcomes.

1.3Superposition and decoherence

In 1935, Schrödinger imagines a cat shut in a box with a radioactive atom, a Geiger counter and a vial of poison. If we apply the theory literally, the cat ends up described by

$$\ket{\psi_\text{cat}} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\ket{\text{dead}} + \ket{\text{alive}}\right).$$

More precisely, the cat is entangled with the atom: the overall state is \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\ket{\text{decayed},\text{dead}} + \ket{\text{intact},\text{alive}}\big)\) (see chapter 4). Schrödinger wanted to show that the Copenhagen interpretation was absurd. The irony is that his cat became the standard illustration of superposition.

Common pitfall — superposition ≠ ignorance
Superposition (the AND)Statistical mixture (the OR)
NatureA single state that combines A and BA or B, but we do not know which
Example\(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{0} + \ket{1})\)Hidden coin: heads or tails
InterferenceYes, the amplitudes combineNo, the probabilities add

Decoherence

A cat is never isolated: air molecules and infrared photons “observe” it constantly. Each interaction carries a little which-path information away into the environment. This is exactly what the slider D in lab 1.1 does: when the information leaks out, the fringe visibility drops to zero and the state behaves like a statistical mixture. For a macroscopic object, this takes a tiny fraction of a second. It is also the first enemy of quantum computers.

The quantum eraser

We “mark” the path of each photon, and the fringes disappear. If we then erase this marking and sort the impacts according to the result of the erasure, the fringes reappear in each subset. This does not allow a message to be sent into the past: without the sorting, done afterwards with information sent by classical means, the screen shows only a blur without fringes.

1.4The qubit

Definition 1.4 — Qubit

A qubit is a two-level quantum system with basis states \(\ket0\) and \(\ket1\). A pure state is written

$$\ket\psi = \alpha\ket0 + \beta\ket1,\qquad |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1,\qquad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C.$$

Up to a global phase (which has no physical effect), we can always write \(\alpha = \cos\frac\theta2\) and \(\beta = e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac\theta2\): a point \((\theta, \varphi)\) on the Bloch sphere. Statistical mixtures (chapter 4) occupy the interior of the sphere.

Clarification

One sometimes reads that a qubit “stores an infinite amount of information” because \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are continuous. This is misleading: no more than one bit of classical information can be extracted from a qubit (the Holevo bound), and a measurement projects the state onto one of the possible outcomes. The power of quantum computing comes from interference between amplitudes, not from reading out “all the results in parallel”.

Hilbert space in three ideas

  • Ket and bra: \(\ket\psi = \binom{\alpha}{\beta}\) and \(\bra\psi = (\alpha^*\ \ \beta^*)\), the Hermitian conjugate.
  • Inner product: \(\braket{\phi|\psi} = \gamma^*\alpha + \delta^*\beta\), which is the amplitude for finding \(\ket\psi\) in the state \(\ket\phi\).
  • Orthonormal basis and completeness: \(\braket{i|j} = \delta_{ij}\) and \(\sum_i \ket i\bra i = \mathbb 1\), hence \(\ket\psi = \sum_i \braket{i|\psi}\ket i\).

Exercise 1.9

The Mach-Zehnder with matrices

★★★

In the basis \(\{\ket x, \ket y\}\), the beam splitter is written \(B = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1 & i\\ i & 1\end{pmatrix}\), the pair of mirrors \(M = \begin{pmatrix}0 & i\\ i & 0\end{pmatrix}\) and the phase plate \(\Phi = \mathrm{diag}(e^{i\varphi}, 1)\). Calculate \(\ket{\psi_\text{out}} = B\,M\,\Phi\,B\ket x\) and recover \(P(x) = \cos^2(\varphi/2)\) (up to the labelling of the outputs).

Hint

Start with \(B\ket x = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\binom{1}{i}\). Keep \(e^{i\varphi}\) factored out until the end, then use \(1 + e^{i\varphi} = 2e^{i\varphi/2}\cos(\varphi/2)\).

Solution

\(\Phi B\ket x = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\binom{e^{i\varphi}}{i}\), then \(M\Phi B\ket x = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\binom{-1}{ie^{i\varphi}}\), and finally

$$B M \Phi B\ket x = \frac12\begin{pmatrix}-1 - e^{i\varphi}\\ -i + ie^{i\varphi}\end{pmatrix} = -e^{i\varphi/2}\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\varphi/2)\\ \sin(\varphi/2)\end{pmatrix}.$$

Hence \(P(x) = \cos^2(\varphi/2)\) and \(P(y) = \sin^2(\varphi/2)\), as in the lecture notes.

·Where do things stand in 2026?

Nobel Prize in Physics 2025

John Clarke, Michel Devoret and John Martinis were awarded the prize “for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit”. Their superconducting circuits of the 1980s are the direct ancestors of the Google and IBM qubits.

Nobel Committee press release, 7 October 2025 — nobelprize.org
Qubits that correct their own errors

In 2024, Google showed on its Willow chip that the error of a logical qubit decreases as the error-correcting code is enlarged: it is divided by about 2.1 each time the code distance is increased. In November 2025, Quantinuum presented Helios (98 barium ions, two-qubit gates with 99.92% fidelity). Take care not to confuse the hundreds of physical qubits in these machines with the few logical qubits that are actually protected.

Nature 638, 920 (2025); Nature 655, 81 (2026)
Interferometry in the field

The Mach-Zehnder formula \(P = \cos^2(\varphi/2)\) lies at the heart of commercially available cold-atom gravimeters, which measure \(g\) to within about \(10^{-8}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\), and of atomic magnetometers for brain imaging without cryogenics.

After the International Year of Quantum

The International Year of Quantum Science and Technology (2025) closed in Accra (Ghana) in February 2026. The UNESCO review notes that activity was more than seven times less dense in Africa than in Europe and North America. Closing this gap also depends on courses like this one.

UNESCO, The Quantum moment: a global report, May 2026
