The postulates set the rules. The state is a normalised vector. Measurable quantities are Hermitian operators. The results are their eigenvalues, with probabilities given by the Born rule. After a measurement, the state is projected. Between two measurements, it evolves according to the Schrödinger equation.

This evolution is unitary: it preserves the norm, and it can be undone. A measurement, on the other hand, cannot be undone.

An eigenstate of the Hamiltonian only picks up a global phase. Nothing observable changes, so we call it a stationary state. For a spin in a magnetic field, the Bloch vector turns around the field. This is Larmor precession.

Now shine an oscillating field at the right frequency on a two-level atom. The probability of finding it excited oscillates between zero and one. These are Rabi oscillations. Their frequency is proportional to the amplitude of the field, not to its intensity. Away from resonance, the oscillation becomes faster and less complete.

These pulses are the logic gates of quantum computers, and the working principle of atomic clocks.

3.1The postulates: the rules of the game

Postulates are not proved. They sum up what chapters 1 and 2 established, and a century of experiments has found no situation that contradicts them. We sort them into two groups: the description of the system, then the measurement.

Postulates 1 to 5 — description and measurement

P1, state. The state is a normalised vector \(\ket\psi\) in a Hilbert space: \(\ket\psi = \sum_n \alpha_n\ket{\varphi_n}\), \(\alpha_n = \braket{\varphi_n|\psi}\), \(\braket{\psi|\psi} = 1\).

P2, observables. Every measurable quantity \(A\) corresponds to a Hermitian operator \(\hat A\).

P3, results. A measurement of \(A\) can only give an eigenvalue \(a_n\): \(\hat A\ket{\varphi_n} = a_n\ket{\varphi_n}\), and \(\hat A = \sum_n a_n\ket{\varphi_n}\bra{\varphi_n}\).

P4, Born rule. \(P(a_n) = |\braket{\varphi_n|\psi}|^2\). On average, \(\langle A\rangle_\psi = \sum_n P(a_n)\,a_n = \bra\psi\hat A\ket\psi\).

P5, projection. After the result \(a_n\), the state becomes

$$\ket{\psi_\text{after}} = \frac{\hat P_n\ket\psi}{\lVert\hat P_n\ket\psi\rVert},\qquad \hat P_n = \ket{\varphi_n}\bra{\varphi_n}.$$

The sixth postulate, the Schrödinger equation, tells how the state changes between two measurements (section 3.2). The lab below runs through the three acts of an experiment on a qubit.

3.2Time evolution and the Schrödinger equation

Postulate 6 — Schrödinger equation

Between two measurements, the state of an isolated system evolves according to

$$i\hbar\,\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\ket{\psi(t)} = \hat H\ket{\psi(t)},$$

where \(\hat H\), the Hamiltonian, is the Hermitian operator associated with the energy. The equation is first order in time: \(\ket{\psi(t_0)}\) fixes \(\ket{\psi(t)}\) for all \(t > t_0\). Chance enters only at the measurement.

Time-evolution operator

If \(\hat H\) does not depend on time, the solution is written with the time-evolution operator:

$$\ket{\psi(t)} = \hat U(t,t_0)\ket{\psi(t_0)},\qquad \hat U(t,t_0) = e^{-i\hat H(t-t_0)/\hbar},\qquad \hat U^\dagger\hat U = \mathbb 1.$$

Unitarity guarantees \(\braket{\psi(t)|\psi(t)} = \braket{\psi(t_0)|\psi(t_0)} = 1\): probabilities stay normalised at all times. It also guarantees reversibility, since \(\hat U^\dagger(t,t_0) = \hat U(t_0,t)\): we “rewind” the evolution by applying \(\hat U^\dagger\).

Stationary states

If \(\hat H\ket{\psi_n} = E_n\ket{\psi_n}\), then \(\ket{\psi_n(t)} = e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}\ket{\psi_n(0)}\). Only a global phase turns: no measurement probability changes. A superposition of states with different energies does evolve, at the rate set by the gaps \((E_n - E_m)/\hbar\).

Expectation values and conserved quantities

Differentiating \(\langle A\rangle = \bra{\psi(t)}\hat A\ket{\psi(t)}\) and using the Schrödinger equation and its conjugate, we obtain, for a time-independent \(\hat A\):

$$\frac{\mathrm d\langle A\rangle}{\mathrm dt} = \frac{1}{i\hbar}\big\langle[\hat A,\hat H]\big\rangle.$$

If \([\hat A,\hat H] = 0\), \(\langle A\rangle\) is constant: \(A\) is a conserved quantity. With \(\hat A = \hat H\), we recover the conservation of the mean energy of an isolated system.

Clarification

The lecture notes call this relation the “Heisenberg equation”. It is rather the Ehrenfest theorem: it concerns expectation values. The Heisenberg equation, \(\mathrm d\hat A_H/\mathrm dt = \tfrac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat A_H,\hat H]\), concerns the operators themselves, in the picture where they are the ones that evolve.

The summary of the lecture notes also states that “only energy is immutable”. This is too restrictive: any quantity that commutes with \(\hat H\) is conserved (momentum of a free particle, \(\hat\sigma_z\) in lab 3.2…). Likewise, “only energy eigenstates are stationary” assumes that any superposition of states with the same energy (a degenerate level) counts as an eigenstate.

Larmor precession

Take \(\hat H = \tfrac{\hbar\omega}{2}\hat\sigma_z\) and \(\ket{\psi(0)} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\ket0 + e^{i\varphi_0}\sin\tfrac\theta2\ket1\). The two components pick up opposite phases \(e^{\mp i\omega t/2}\); up to a global phase,

$$\ket{\psi(t)} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\ket0 + e^{i(\varphi_0+\omega t)}\sin\tfrac\theta2\ket1 .$$

θ does not change and φ grows at rate ω: the Bloch vector turns around z. It follows that \(\langle\sigma_x\rangle(t) = \sin\theta\cos(\varphi_0+\omega t)\), \(\langle\sigma_y\rangle(t) = \sin\theta\sin(\varphi_0+\omega t)\) and \(\langle\sigma_z\rangle = \cos\theta\), constant since \([\hat\sigma_z,\hat H] = 0\).

The quantum Zeno effect

Measuring a system often can slow down its evolution. For a short time, the probability of leaving the initial state grows as \(t^2\), not as \(t\). If the interval is cut into \(N\) pieces separated by measurements, each piece contributes \((t/N)^2\), that is \(N(t/N)^2 = t^2/N\) in total: the leakage tends to zero as \(N\) grows.

Clarification

The lecture notes illustrate the effect with an atom that has “a 50% chance of decaying in 1 s”, measured every microsecond. Two caveats. First, a decay follows an exponential law: the \(t^2\) regime exists only at the very first instants, far shorter than a microsecond, and measurements every microsecond do not change the half-life. Second, the state \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{\text{intact}} + \ket{\text{decayed}})\) is a simplification: the decay product becomes entangled with the emitted particle. The reference experiment (Itano et al., NIST, 1990) used a driven Rabi transition in trapped ions, exactly the situation of exercise 3.5.

Exercise 3.5

Zeno in two steps

★★☆

Two levels \(\ket A, \ket B\), with \(\bra A\hat H\ket A = \bra B\hat H\ket B = 0\) and \(\bra B\hat H\ket A = V\) real. We start from \(\ket A\) and use \(\hat U(\delta t) \approx \mathbb 1 - \tfrac{i}{\hbar}\hat H\delta t\). \(P_1\) is the probability of finding \(\ket B\) after \(\delta t\) without measurement; \(P_2\) is the same probability if we measure in \(\{\ket A, \ket B\}\) at \(\delta t/2\). What is \(P_2/P_1\) to the lowest non-zero order?

Solution

\(\hat U(\delta t)\ket A \approx \ket A - \tfrac{iV\delta t}{\hbar}\ket B\), so \(P_1 = (V\delta t/\hbar)^2\). With the measurement: at \(\delta t/2\), we find \(\ket B\) with probability \(p = (V\delta t/2\hbar)^2\) and stay there to first order; otherwise we restart from \(\ket A\) and gain another \(p\). Total: \(P_2 \approx 2p = P_1/2\). With \(N\) measurements, \(P_N \approx P_1/N\).

Analogy to critique — the watchful parent

A parent keeps asking their child: “You haven’t moved, have you?” In the end, the child stays frozen. The lecture notes compare the Zeno effect to this constant attention that freezes the evolution.

Where does the analogy break down?

The child freezes out of fear: that is a force, an added influence. In the Zeno effect, the measurement exerts no push; it projects the state, and the \(t^2\) growth of the probabilities does the rest. If the probability grew linearly, as for an ordinary decay, measuring often would change nothing. Finally, each measurement has a small chance of finding the child… already gone: the freeze is never total.

3.3The qubit’s dance: Rabi oscillations

A qubit has two levels, \(\ket0\) (ground) and \(\ket1\) (excited), separated by \(\hbar\omega_0\): \(\hat H_0 = E_0\ket0\bra0 + E_1\ket1\bra1\). An oscillating field of angular frequency \(\omega\) close to \(\omega_0\) (microwave, laser, radio frequency) adds a coupling \(\hat V(t)\) between the two levels. Moving to the frame that rotates with the field, and neglecting the terms that oscillate at \(\omega + \omega_0\), we obtain a time-independent Hamiltonian:

$$\tilde H = \frac{\hbar}{2}\big(\Omega\,\hat\sigma_x + \Delta\,\hat\sigma_z\big),\qquad \Omega' = \sqrt{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}.$$

\(\Omega\) is the Rabi angular frequency (set by the field amplitude), \(\Delta\) the detuning \(\pm(\omega-\omega_0)\), whose sign depends on the convention. As for Larmor, the Bloch vector turns, this time around the axis \((\Omega, 0, \Delta)\), at angular frequency \(\Omega'\).

Rabi formula

Starting from \(\ket0\) at \(t = 0\),

$$P_{1\leftarrow0}(t) = \frac{\Omega^2}{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}\,\sin^2\!\Big(\frac{\sqrt{\Omega^2+\Delta^2}}{2}\,t\Big).$$

At resonance (\(\Delta = 0\)), the transfer is complete. A π pulse (\(t = \pi/\Omega\)) sends \(\ket0\) to \(-i\ket1\): it is an X gate up to a global phase. A π/2 pulse (\(t = \pi/2\Omega\)) gives \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket0 - i\ket1) = \ket{-i}\).

Clarification

The Rabi frequency is proportional to the amplitude of the field (\(\Omega = \gamma B_1\) for a spin, \(\Omega = dE_0/\hbar\) for an electric dipole), not to its intensity, which varies as the square of the amplitude.

The lecture notes list errors in pulse duration and frequency among the sources of “decoherence”. These are coherent errors: a rotation that lasts slightly too long is still a unitary evolution, reversible and correctable by calibration. Decoherence refers to the loss of information to the environment, which no pulse can undo.

Analogy to critique — the talking drum and resonance

The master drummer makes a distant drum vibrate by striking his own at the right frequency. In the same way, a field at the resonance frequency moves the atom from one level to the other.

Where does the analogy break down?

The distant drum is a classical oscillator: its vibration amplitude takes any value and we see it directly. The qubit has only two levels. What oscillates is a probability; each measurement gives 0 or 1, never “half excited”. And under a sustained field, the qubit does not just go up: it comes back down, gets excited again, and so on. The drum, for its part, is not “de-excited” by the strikes that set it vibrating.

From Rabi oscillations to clocks: the Ramsey method

An atomic clock locks an oscillator to the frequency \(\omega_0\) of a transition. It needs a resonance that is as narrow as possible. With a single π pulse, the width is of order \(\Omega\). The Ramsey method separates two π/2 pulses by a free precession of duration \(T\): the accumulated phase \(\Delta T\) gives \(P_1 \approx \cos^2(\Delta T/2)\), fringes of width \(1/(2T)\) in frequency: the longer \(T\), the finer the fringes.

Clarification

The lecture notes state that “the second is defined as 9,192,631,770 Rabi oscillations of caesium”. This is incorrect. Since 1967, the second has been the duration of 9,192,631,770 periods of the radiation corresponding to the hyperfine transition of the ground state of caesium 133. The Rabi frequency, by contrast, depends on the amplitude of the applied field: it cannot serve as a standard. Rabi oscillations (and above all Ramsey fringes) serve only to locate the resonance.

Exercise 3.3.3 of the lecture notes also gives \(\Delta E = 4.135\,667\times10^{-6}\) eV, which leads to 1 GHz and not 9.19 GHz (see exercise 3.9). The correct value is \(\Delta E \approx 3.80\times10^{-5}\) eV.

For MRI (exercise 3.3.2 of the lecture notes), note that at 1.5 T and body temperature, the excess of “aligned” protons is only a few parts per million. The signal comes from the precession of the magnetisation of this small excess, detected by induction in a coil; it is not a projective measurement of individual spins.

The MRI “π/2” pulse

★★☆

The spins start in \(\ket0\). We apply \(R_y(\pi/2) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\mathbb 1 - i\hat Y)\), with \(\hat Y = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -i\\ i & 0\end{pmatrix}\). (a) Compute the final state and place it on the Bloch sphere. (b) What are the probabilities of obtaining \(\pm\hbar/2\) for \(S_z\)? (c) Same question for \(S_x\), whose eigenstates are \(\ket\pm\).

Hint

\(-i\hat Y = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -1\\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}\) is real: no complex number will appear.

Solution

(a) \(R_y(\pi/2)\ket0 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\binom{1}{1} = \ket+\), on the equator, along +x. (b) 50% and 50%. (c) \(+\hbar/2\) with certainty, since the state is \(\ket+\) itself. Measurements in Z cannot tell \(\ket+\) from a 50/50 mixture; a measurement in X can. In lab 3.1, prepare \(\ket0\), choose \(\hat\sigma_x\) and 90°: you get \(\ket{-i}\), because a rotation about x and a rotation about y do not lead to the same point on the equator.

·Where do things stand in 2026?

Quantum Echoes: U, then U†

In October 2025, Google published in Nature a measurement of out-of-time-order correlators on 103 qubits of its Willow chip. The protocol evolves the system “forwards” with \(\hat U\), perturbs one qubit, then makes it “come back” with \(\hat U^\dagger\): exactly the “Rewind” button of lab 3.1, on a much larger machine. Google reports a computation 13,000 times faster than the best known classical algorithm. This factor is a claim relative to current algorithms: it may shrink if better classical methods appear.

Google Research, 22 October 2025 — research.google/blog/a-verifiable-quantum-advantage
Clocks: from the atom to the nucleus

NIST’s single aluminium-ion clock reaches a relative systematic uncertainty of \(5.5\times10^{-19}\) (2025). On 7 October 2026, two teams (TU Wien and a Chinese team) published the first nuclear clocks in Nature: a vacuum-ultraviolet laser, near 148 nm, is locked to a transition of the thorium-229 nucleus embedded in a crystal. Their stability is still below that of the best atomic clocks. Optical clocks already outperform caesium fountains, but the redefinition of the second is not expected before the 2030 General Conference on Weights and Measures.

NIST, July 2025; Nature, 7 October 2026; BIPM, FAQ on the redefinition of the second
Larmor precession at the service of the brain

Optically pumped atomic magnetometers measure a magnetic field through the precession of polarised atomic spins, the phenomenon of lab 3.2. They make wearable magnetoencephalography possible, without the cryogenic cooling that SQUIDs require.

2025 review of OPM-MEG — pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12320009
A 2026 Nobel Prize linked to non-stationary states

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics rewards Francis Halzen for the IceCube neutrino observatory. It is not a quantum computing prize, but it touches this chapter: a neutrino created with a given “flavour” is a superposition of states with different masses, hence different energies. This state is not stationary, and its flavour oscillates as it propagates.

Nobel Committee press release, 6 October 2026 — nobelprize.org
