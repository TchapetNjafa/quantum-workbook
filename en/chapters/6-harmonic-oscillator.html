A mass on a spring, a vibrating molecule, a mode of the electromagnetic field: all of them obey the same Hamiltonian.

We solve it with two ladder operators. The creation operator, a dagger, moves up one rung; the annihilation operator, a, moves down one. Their commutator equals one. The energies are then h-bar omega times n plus one half: equally spaced levels.

The lowest level is not zero. It equals one half h-bar omega. This is the zero-point energy, imposed by the uncertainty relation. A particle at rest exactly at the bottom of the well would have a perfectly known position and momentum.

The wave functions of the Fock states have n nodes, and they spill slightly into the regions forbidden to a classical particle.

Coherent states are the closest to classical motion. Their wave packet oscillates in the well without changing shape. The number of quanta follows a Poisson distribution, whose mean is the modulus of alpha, squared. This is the state of laser light. Gravitational-wave detectors go further: they inject squeezed light, which reduces the noise on one quantity at the cost of the other, without ever violating Heisenberg.

6.1Energy quantisation

Classically, a particle of mass \(m\) pulled back by a force \(F = -kx\) oscillates at the angular frequency \(\omega = \sqrt{k/m}\). Its energy can take any positive value. It moves back and forth between two turning points \(\pm x_0\), and it is near these edges, where it slows down, that we are most likely to find it.

Definitions 6.1 — Ladder operators

With the length \(\ell = \sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}\), we set \(\hat X = X/\ell\) and \(\hat P = \ell P/\hbar\), which satisfy \([\hat X, \hat P] = i\). Then

$$H = \frac{P^2}{2m} + \frac12 m\omega^2 X^2 = \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\left(\hat X^2 + \hat P^2\right),\qquad a = \frac{\hat X + i\hat P}{\sqrt2},\quad a^\dagger = \frac{\hat X - i\hat P}{\sqrt2}.$$

We find \([a, a^\dagger] = 1\) and \(H = \hbar\omega\left(N + \tfrac12\right)\) with the Hermitian number operator \(N = a^\dagger a\). \(a\) and \(a^\dagger\) are not Hermitian: they are not observables.

Principle 6.1 — Spectrum of the oscillator
$$a\ket n = \sqrt n\,\ket{n-1},\qquad a\ket0 = 0,\qquad a^\dagger\ket n = \sqrt{n+1}\,\ket{n+1},\qquad \ket n = \frac{(a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket0.$$

The energies are \(E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \tfrac12\right)\), \(n = 0, 1, 2,\dots\): equally spaced rungs, separated by \(\hbar\omega\). The lowest one, \(E_0 = \tfrac12\hbar\omega\), is the zero-point energy.

Clarification — where does the zero-point energy come from?

The lecture notes attribute it to a “principle of minimal action”. The name is misleading: the principle of least action is a classical variational principle, unrelated to this. The correct argument uses the Heisenberg inequality. In an eigenstate, \(\langle X\rangle = \langle P\rangle = 0\), so

$$\begin{aligned}\langle H\rangle &= \frac{(\Delta P)^2}{2m} + \frac12 m\omega^2(\Delta X)^2\\ &\ge \frac{\hbar^2}{8m(\Delta X)^2} + \frac12 m\omega^2(\Delta X)^2 \ \ge\ \frac12\hbar\omega,\end{aligned}$$

the minimum being reached for \((\Delta X)^2 = \hbar/2m\omega\), that is, by the Gaussian \(\psi_0\).

In 1900, Planck derived the black-body law by assuming that the material oscillators of the cavity wall exchange energy only in multiples of \(h\nu\). At first he saw this as a calculational device. It was Einstein, in 1905, who assigned quanta to light itself. Planck’s levels were \(nh\nu\): the half-quantum of the zero point appears only with the quantum mechanics of 1925–1926.

6.1.3Fock states in the position representation

With the reduced variable \(u = x/\ell\), the operator \(a\) becomes \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(u + \tfrac{d}{du}\right)\). The condition \(a\ket0 = 0\) is then a first-order equation, \(\psi_0' = -u\,\psi_0\), whose solution is a Gaussian. The excited states are obtained by applying \(a^\dagger\).

Eigenfunctions 6.1.3
$$\psi_n(u) = \frac{1}{\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{2^n n!}}\,H_n(u)\,e^{-u^2/2},$$

with \(H_0 = 1\), \(H_1 = 2u\) and the recurrence

$$H_{n+1} = 2u\,H_n - 2n\,H_{n-1}\quad (n\ge1).$$

\(\psi_n\) has \(n\) nodes and the parity of \(n\): \(\psi_n(-u) = (-1)^n\psi_n(u)\). The normalisation is with respect to \(u\); in the variable \(x\), divide by \(\sqrt\ell\).

Clarification

The lecture notes state the Hermite recurrence “for \(n \ge 2\)”. It holds from \(n = 1\) onwards: it gives \(H_2 = 2u\cdot 2u - 2 = 4u^2 - 2\), which could not be obtained otherwise from \(H_0\) and \(H_1\).

Superposing two levels

A Fock state is stationary: its density does not move. A superposition of two levels, on the other hand, evolves. Each component rotates at its own frequency \(E_n/\hbar\), and only the difference matters:

$$\begin{aligned}\left|\psi(x,t)\right|^2 &= \tfrac12\left(\psi_n^2 + \psi_m^2\right)\\ &\quad + \psi_n\psi_m\cos\big((m-n)\,\omega t\big).\end{aligned}$$

6.2Quasi-classical states: coherent states

In every Fock state, \(\langle X\rangle = 0\). Yet a laser beam, or a pendulum, oscillates visibly. We therefore need states that mix several \(\ket n\) with well-tuned phases. Schrödinger wrote them down in 1926; Glauber made them the foundation of quantum optics in the 1960s (Nobel Prize 2005).

Definition 6.2 — Coherent state

\(\ket\alpha\) is an eigenstate of the annihilation operator, \(a\ket\alpha = \alpha\ket\alpha\) with \(\alpha\in\mathbb C\):

$$\ket\alpha = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket n = D(\alpha)\ket0.$$

The displacement operator \(D(\alpha) = e^{\alpha a^\dagger - \alpha^* a}\) is unitary. \(\ket\alpha\) is therefore the vacuum, displaced without deformation to the point \(\big(\langle\hat X\rangle, \langle\hat P\rangle\big) = \sqrt2\,(\mathrm{Re}\,\alpha, \mathrm{Im}\,\alpha)\) of phase space.

Properties 6.2
  • Counting: \(P(n) = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\,|\alpha|^{2n}/n!\) (Poisson distribution), \(\langle N\rangle = |\alpha|^2\), \(\Delta N = |\alpha|\).
  • Minimum uncertainty: \(\Delta x = \ell/\sqrt2\), \(\Delta p = \hbar/(\ell\sqrt2)\), \(\Delta x\,\Delta p = \hbar/2\), as for the vacuum.
  • Evolution: \(\ket{\alpha(t)} = e^{-i\omega t/2}\,\ket{\alpha e^{-i\omega t}}\). The Gaussian packet follows the classical trajectory without spreading.
Clarifications
  • The lecture notes say that the Poisson distribution is “maximal at \(\langle N\rangle\)”. Its maximum is at \(n = \lfloor|\alpha|^2\rfloor\) (and also at \(|\alpha|^2 - 1\) when \(|\alpha|^2\) is an integer); it coincides with \(\langle N\rangle\) only to within one unit.
  • From \(\Delta H = \hbar\omega|\alpha|\), one cannot deduce that an external force turns the vacuum into a coherent state. The result is true for an imposed classical force, but it is proved separately, by solving the evolution under \(H - F(t)X\).
  • Squeezed states do not “cheat” Heisenberg: they respect \(\Delta\hat X\,\Delta\hat P \ge \tfrac12\) and reduce the noise on one quadrature by increasing it on the other. Among minimum-uncertainty states, coherent states are those whose two reduced uncertainties are equal.

Analogy to critique — the rhythmic trance

The lecture notes compare the coherent state to ceremonies in which a steady rhythm, played without interruption, carries a whole group along: gnawa music from Morocco, vodun ceremonies from Benin. A regular drum, a shared phase, an oscillation that keeps its shape.

Where does the analogy break down?

The dancers are individuals who synchronise by interacting. A coherent state describes a single oscillator mode; its stability does not come from any interaction between photons, which hardly interact with one another. The analogy captures the idea of a stable phase well, but not that of an uncertain number of quanta: you can count the dancers, whereas a measurement of \(N\) on \(\ket\alpha\) gives a random result. A geographical note: gnawa music is Moroccan (North Africa); vodun comes from the Gulf of Guinea (Benin, Togo), from where it reached Haiti.

Exercise 6.10

The field of a laser: Fock or coherent?

★★★

One mode of the electric field is written \(E = i\xi\left(a\,e^{-i\omega t} - a^\dagger e^{i\omega t}\right)\), with \(\xi\) real. (1) Show that \(\langle E\rangle = 0\) in every state \(\ket n\). (2) Compute \(\langle E\rangle\) in \(\ket\alpha\) with \(\alpha = |\alpha|e^{i\theta}\). (3) Show that \((\Delta E)^2 = \xi^2\) in \(\ket\alpha\), whatever \(\alpha\), and comment on \(\Delta E/|\langle E\rangle|_{\max}\) for an intense laser.

Hint

For (1), \(\langle n|a|n\rangle = \langle n|a^\dagger|n\rangle = 0\). For (3), rewrite \(\langle\alpha|a^\dagger a|\alpha\rangle\) and \(\langle\alpha|aa^\dagger|\alpha\rangle = |\alpha|^2 + 1\).

Solution

(1) \(a\) and \(a^\dagger\) change \(n\) by one unit, so their mean values in \(\ket n\) are zero. A Fock state has a definite amplitude but no phase: the mean field is zero.

(2) \(\langle E\rangle = i\xi\left(\alpha e^{-i\omega t} - \alpha^* e^{i\omega t}\right) = 2\xi|\alpha|\sin(\omega t - \theta)\): a sinusoidal wave of amplitude \(2\xi|\alpha|\), with its phase set by \(\theta\).

(3) \(\langle E^2\rangle = -\xi^2\langle(ae^{-i\omega t} - a^\dagger e^{i\omega t})^2\rangle = \xi^2\left[2|\alpha|^2 + 1 - \alpha^2e^{-2i\omega t} - \alpha^{*2}e^{2i\omega t}\right]\), and subtracting \(\langle E\rangle^2\) removes everything except \(\xi^2\). Hence \(\Delta E = \xi\), the same value as in the vacuum, and \(\Delta E/(2\xi|\alpha|) = 1/(2\sqrt{\langle N\rangle})\to 0\) for an intense beam: the field becomes classical.

Clarification — Casimir effect

The lecture notes describe the vacuum as filled with “virtual pairs that appear and disappear”. This is a popular-science image, not an observation. The calculation rests on the sum of the energies \(\tfrac12\hbar\omega\) of the modes allowed between the plates. In 1D, \(E(L) = -\pi\hbar c/(24L)\) gives \(F = -dE/dL = -\pi\hbar c/(24L^2)\): an attractive force.

·Where do things stand in 2026?

2025 Nobel Prize in Physics: a quantised electrical oscillator

John Clarke, Michel Devoret and John Martinis were awarded the prize “for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit”. In their experiments of the 1980s, a superconducting circuit with a Josephson junction shows discrete energy levels, like the oscillator of this chapter. The junction makes the oscillator anharmonic: the rungs are no longer equally spaced, which makes it possible to address only the first two. This is the principle of today’s superconducting qubits.

Press release of the Nobel Committee, 7 October 2025 — nobelprize.org
LIGO squeezes quantum noise

The shot noise of a laser is that of the coherent state: \(\Delta N = \sqrt{\langle N\rangle}\). Since May 2023, LIGO has been injecting into its interferometers a squeezed vacuum whose noise-ellipse orientation depends on frequency, thanks to a 300 m filter cavity. Quantum noise drops across the whole band: shot noise at high frequency, radiation pressure at low frequency. The expected gain is about 60% more mergers detected.

LIGO, press release of 23 October 2023; Phys. Rev. X (2023)
Results of the O4 run

The fourth observing run of LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA ended on 18 November 2025, after two and a half years. About 250 candidate signals were identified in real time, and the analysis of the first third has already yielded 128 significant events, compared with 90 for the three previous runs combined. The signal GW250114, received on 14 January 2025, has a signal-to-noise ratio of 80 and made it possible to test Hawking’s area theorem. An intermediate six-month run is planned from November 2026, a schedule that may change.

LIGO, 18 November 2025; Phys. Rev. Lett., September 2025; observing.docs.ligo.org/plan
A 16-microgram Schrödinger cat

At ETH Zurich, a 16 µg mechanical resonator, about \(10^{17}\) atoms, was prepared in superpositions of motional states, known as “Schrödinger cat” states. This resonator is a macroscopic quantum harmonic oscillator: it displays the Fock states and coherent states of this chapter, and their superpositions.

Bild et al., Science (2023), ETH Zurich
